## 2026-05-04
- **Ingested week 11.** Created: `weeks/week-11.md`, `concepts/validation.md`, `concepts/cross-validation.md`, `concepts/learning-principles.md`. Updated `index.md`. Two-part week: (1) Validation as the second cure for overfitting (the first being regularisation from week 10). Hold out $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}} \subset \mathcal{D}$ of size $K$, train on the rest, evaluate on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$. $E_{\text{val}}(g^-)$ is an unbiased estimate of $E_{\text{out}}(g^-)$ with variance $\sigma_{\text{val}}^2 = \sigma^2(g^-)/K \leq 1/(4K)$ for binary classification (Bernoulli per-example error, $p(1-p) \leq 1/4$). The K trade-off: large $K$ → low variance but $g^-$ trained on too few examples; small $K$ → $g^- \approx g$ but noisy estimate. Rule of thumb $K = N/5$. After model selection picks $\mathcal{H}_{m^\ast}$, retrain on full $N$ — $E_{\text{out}}(g_{m^\ast}) \leq E_{\text{out}}(g_{m^\ast}^-)$. Validation generalisation bound: $E_{\text{out}}(g_{m^\ast}) \leq E_{\text{val}}(g_{m^\ast}^-) + O(\sqrt{\log M / K})$ — only $\log M$ because $M$ is a small finite candidate list. Validation vs regularisation: both estimate the same overfit penalty equation $E_{\text{out}} = E_{\text{in}} + \text{penalty}$, regularisation estimates the penalty term, validation estimates $E_{\text{out}}$ directly. Cross-validation removes the $K$ trade-off by using all data: LOOCV ($V = N$, almost unbiased for $\bar{E}_{\text{out}}(N-1)$, $N$ trainings — closed form for linear regression via hat matrix), V-fold ($V = 10$ practical default). (2) Three learning principles closing the module's theory: **Occam's Razor** — simpler hypothesis sets (small $\Omega(\mathcal{H})$, small VC dimension) generalise better; "better" means lower $E_{\text{out}}$, not aesthetic elegance. **Sampling Bias** — if $P_{\text{train}} \neq P_{\text{deploy}}$, the i.i.d. assumption fails and the VC bound is silent; no algorithm rescues a model trained on the wrong distribution. **Data Snooping** — any test-set influence on any decision (preprocessing, feature engineering, hyperparameter selection) contaminates it; reported $E_{\text{test}}$ is then biased downward. Discipline: lock $\mathcal{D}_{\text{test}}$ in the safe, evaluate once at the end. Lec 2's recap of weeks 7–10 (probabilistic ML, VC vs bias-variance, OLS = MLE, MAP = ridge) was not duplicated — already covered by existing concept pages.

## 2026-05-04
- **Ingested week 10.** Created: `weeks/week-10.md`, `concepts/overfitting.md`, `concepts/regularization.md`, `concepts/lasso-regression.md`. Updated `index.md`. Two-part week: (1) Overfitting — low $E_{\text{in}}$ but high $E_{\text{out}}$. Six common causes (model complexity, data scarcity, too many epochs, no regularisation, high-variance features, poor data processing). The "two-learners" experiment: even when both learners know the target is degree-10, $\mathcal{H}_2$ beats $\mathcal{H}_{10}$ on test error if $N$ small — the right hypothesis class isn't enough; need data to constrain it. Stochastic noise (label randomness $\epsilon$) and deterministic noise (target complexity beyond $\mathcal{H}$) are operationally equivalent — both encourage overfitting. MLE is structurally unable to prevent this because it has no preference for simpler hypotheses. (2) Regularisation as the structural cure. Built up from hard constraint ($w_3 = \cdots = w_{10} = 0$), to looser constraint (sparsity: $\geq 8$ of $w_q = 0$), to soft constraint $\mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{w} \leq C$. Lagrangian transforms constrained → unconstrained: augmented error $E_{\text{aug}} = E_{\text{in}} + (\lambda/N) \mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{w}$, equivalent for paired $C, \lambda$. Closed-form solution $\mathbf{w}_{\text{REG}} = (\mathbf{Z}^\top \mathbf{Z} + \lambda \mathbf{I})^{-1} \mathbf{Z}^\top \mathbf{y}$ — recovers ridge regression from week 8 by a different derivation route (constrained optimisation rather than Bayesian MAP). Effective VC dimension $d_{\text{EFF}}(\mathcal{H}, \mathcal{A}) = d_{\text{VC}}(\mathcal{H}(C))$ smaller than nominal because the algorithm only navigates within the ball. L1 (lasso) replaces the round constraint ball with a corner-bearing diamond, producing sparse solutions; corresponds to Laplace prior in Bayesian view. Choosing $\lambda$ depends on $\sigma^2$, target complexity, $N$ — none observable, hence cross-validation (next week).

## 2026-05-04
- **Ingested week 9.** Created: `weeks/week-09.md`, `concepts/dichotomy.md`, `concepts/growth-function.md`, `concepts/break-point.md`, `concepts/vc-dimension.md`, `concepts/bias-variance-decomposition.md`, `concepts/learning-curve.md`. Updated `index.md`. Two-part week: (1) Replacing $M$ in the generalisation bound with a finite quantity. Dichotomies count distinct labellings on $N$ specific inputs ($\leq 2^N$). The growth function $m_{\mathcal{H}}(N)$ takes the max over inputs. A break point $k$ — smallest $N$ with $m_{\mathcal{H}}(k) < 2^k$ — forces $m_{\mathcal{H}}(N)$ to grow polynomially: $m_{\mathcal{H}}(N) \leq N^{d_{\text{VC}}} + 1$. The VC dimension $d_{\text{VC}}$ equals the largest shatterable $N$, equivalently break point minus one. For perceptron in $\mathbb{R}^d$: $d_{\text{VC}} = d + 1$ (number of free parameters). The VC bound $\mathbb{P}[|E_{\text{in}} - E_{\text{out}}| > \epsilon] \leq 4 m_{\mathcal{H}}(2N) e^{-\epsilon^2 N/8}$ contracts as $N \to \infty$ for finite $d_{\text{VC}}$. Sample complexity: theory says $N \approx 10{,}000 d_{\text{VC}}$, practice says $N \approx 10 d_{\text{VC}}$. SVMs achieve generalisation by margin-restricting to fat hyperplanes: $d_{\text{VC}}(\rho) \leq \lceil R^2/\rho^2 \rceil + 1$, independent of $d$. (2) Bias–variance as the average-case complement to VC's worst case. Decomposition $\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}[E_{\text{out}}] = \text{bias} + \text{var} + \sigma^2$ for squared loss. Bias = how far average hypothesis $\bar{g}$ is from $f$; variance = how much $g^{(\mathcal{D})}$ fluctuates around $\bar{g}$. Optimal model complexity depends on $N$ — the $\sin(\pi x)$ example shows constant beats line at $N = 2$ but loses at $N = 5$. Learning curves visualise the decomposition; for linear regression, exact form $E_{\text{in}} = \sigma^2(1 - (d+1)/N)$, $E_{\text{out}} = \sigma^2(1 + (d+1)/N)$, gap decays as $1/N$.

Updated `index.md`. Two-part week: (1) Bayesian regression — Gaussian prior + Gaussian likelihood → closed-form Gaussian posterior; MAP estimate is exactly ridge regression, so L2 regularisation is the negative log of a Gaussian prior (regularisation = prior). (2) Learning theory — Hoeffding's inequality bounds $|E_{\text{in}} - E_{\text{out}}|$ with probability $\geq 1 - 2e^{-2\epsilon^2 N}$; union bound over $M$ hypotheses gives the generalization bound $E_{\text{out}} \leq E_{\text{in}} + \sqrt{(1/2N)\log(2M/\delta)}$. The two opposing forces ($M$ small for tight bound vs $M$ large for good fit) formalise the bias-variance trade-off. Sets up VC dimension via the dichotomy/effective-$M$ idea.
